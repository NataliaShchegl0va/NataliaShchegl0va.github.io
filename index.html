Experimentation · Ad tech

Did a new targeting feature really lift app installs?

50/50 A/B test · 30 advertiser customers · 14 days
Wilcoxon signed-rank · p < 0.001
Context
An ad-tech company's ML engineers built a feature meant to increase view-through installs for advertised apps. It was tested against control before going to production.
Method
Every customer had both a control and an experiment arm, so I used a paired design. The differences were not normally distributed, even after removing outliers, so instead of a paired t-test I used the non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank test on each customer's average daily difference.
Result
The feature significantly increased installs. The customers with the largest gains in view-through installs were not the ones flagged as sensitive to view-through attribution.
Takeaway
Roll out: the lift is real and the rollout risk is low.

Media investment · Response curves

How much should we spend before returns flatten out?

Hill-curve fit · profit-maximizing spend $1,677
expected revenue $2,909 · expected profit $1,232
Context
Advertising has diminishing returns: past some point, each extra dollar brings in less revenue. This is the same question I answer with Media Mix Modelling at work, shown here on a simulated spend and revenue dataset.
Method
I compared quadratic, logarithmic and Hill (S-curve) models. The Hill function fits best because it captures saturation. I then defined "optimal" two ways: where marginal revenue equals marginal cost, and where profit peaks.
Result
The efficiency point sits near $1,067 of spend. Profit peaks at $1,677, returning about $2,909 in revenue.
Takeaway
Spend up to the profit peak, or stop at the efficiency point if every extra dollar must pay for itself. Which definition applies is a business choice, and the curve makes the trade-off visible.

Customer segmentation · Tableau

Where should a chemical maker focus, and what should it drop?

Grow: Machinery segment, Chemical E
Divest: Aerospace Defense, Chemical A
Context
A chemical manufacturer was losing key customers and market share to technology-led startups and needed a short-term action plan.
Method
I built a Tableau story on the company's sales data covering what customers buy, where they buy, which industries bring the most revenue, and how profit margin and the number of potential customers vary by segment and product.
Result
Paper & Packaging brings the most revenue ($21.43M, 4.4× the next segment) but has the lowest margin. Machinery has the highest margin and the largest pool of potential customers. Aerospace Defense has the smallest market, about 1.7K businesses, and the second-lowest margin. Chemical E has the highest margin and is bought across most industries.
Takeaway
Focus on winning Machinery customers and on Chemical E. Move resources away from Aerospace Defense and stop selling Chemical A. Keep investing in Paper & Packaging for now, including Chemical K, which few other customers buy but which that segment depends on.

Classification · E-commerce

Can we predict which orders will be cancelled?

4 models compared · Decision Tree selected
recall 0.98 on cancelled orders
Context
Cancelled orders cost an online retailer 9% of its sales in one quarter and disrupt fulfillment. I used Amazon India sales data for Q2 2022.
Method
Only 14% of orders were cancelled, so I judged models on recall rather than accuracy. I compared logistic regression, KNN with SMOTE resampling, a decision tree and a random forest, then tuned the tree with Bayesian optimization.
Result
The decision tree caught 98% of cancellations. Free shipping, free financing and the absence of any promotion were the strongest signals, followed by the specific product.
Takeaway
Move orders the model flags to the end of the fulfillment queue, and investigate the products with the highest cancellation rates for quality or stock issues.

Forecasting · Ride-hailing

How many ride requests will a zone get in the next hour?

SARIMAX vs linear regression vs XGBoost
tuned XGBoost · R² 0.76
Context
Matching drivers to demand improves service and revenue. I used 2021 New York for-hire trip data, about 12 million requests a month, joined to daily weather.
Method
I compared SARIMAX with weather as exogenous variables, linear regression with lagged demand, and XGBoost, working in Colab with the data stored in GCP. A model using the previous hour's demand reached R² 0.91, but that input is costly to supply in real time, so I chose a tuned XGBoost model with a log-transformed target.
Result
Hour of day, day of week, month and demand at the same hour the day before drive the forecast. Weather plays a moderate role, led by humidity.
Takeaway
Use the model for driver scheduling, and train separate models for zone types such as airports and tourist areas.
